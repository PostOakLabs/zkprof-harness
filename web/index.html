zkprof-web

Browser-native profiling for zero-knowledge proving. Two pages, one schema, nothing measured until you press a button.

Pages

Harness

One Groth16/BN254 prover, two circuits to point it at — Merkle-Poseidon, and the SHA-256 preimage circuit csp-benchmarks measures — compiled to wasm32-unknown-unknown in two profiles, scalar and +simd128, run in your browser and reported as schema-valid JSON with time attributed per cryptographic primitive.

Marshalling bench

What it costs to cross the JavaScript ↔ WebAssembly boundary, and how much of that cost goes away if you batch across it: one Poseidon compression per crossing, then batched ×8, ×32 and ×128, against a pure-copy baseline.

Two deployment contexts

Browsers coarsen performance.now() to blunt timing side channels, and the coarsening depends on whether the page is cross-origin isolated: roughly 5 µs when it is, roughly 100 µs when it is not. That is a 20× difference in the resolution of every number here, so it is part of the measurement rather than a deployment detail.

Both contexts are runnable on purpose: python web/server.py sends Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin and Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: require-corp, and --no-isolation sends neither. Every report records which context it came from, and which clock resolution it measured.

Nothing in this repository is a published benchmark. Every figure these pages produce is measured in the browser that opened them, at the moment a button was pressed.
